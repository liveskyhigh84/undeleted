Recovery guide

Todoist lost my tasks. How do I get them back?

Work through these steps in order. The first two cost nothing and take a minute. If you're on a paid plan, Todoist's own backups will probably get you most of the way.

Published 17 October 2026 by the maker of UnDeleted, a backup tool mentioned at the end. Todoist details come from Todoist's help center as of that date; check it for current menu names.

1. Make sure the tasks are actually gone

A lot of "lost" tasks are hidden, not deleted. Before anything else:

  • Look for an archived project. Todoist's help center says to check whether a project was archived before assuming it was deleted. Archived projects drop out of your sidebar but still exist.
  • Check filters and views. A filter, a collapsed section, or a project switched to board view can make tasks look missing.
  • Search for a word from the task. If search finds it, it's still there.
  • Check completed tasks. A recurring task, or one finished by accident on a shared project, shows up as completed rather than deleted.

2. Use Reporting (the activity log) to find out what happened

On web or desktop, click your avatar and choose Reporting. For a single project, open its three-dots menu and pick the Activity tab. For a single task, its three-dots menu has View activity.

Reporting records task changes (adds, edits, completions) plus edits to projects and labels, and new or changed comments. It tells you when something changed and, on shared projects, who changed it.

What it won't do: Reporting is view-only. You can't restore anything from it. The free Beginner plan shows seven days of history, and tasks inside a deleted project don't appear in it as deleted tasks.

Use it to get a date. You'll need that date in the next step.

3. Restore from Todoist's built-in backups (Pro and Business)

Todoist has no undo for deleting a task. Paid plans do get automatic backups, though:

  • A backup is made at midnight in your time zone on any day your account changed. You can also make one by hand with Create backup.
  • Up to 21 backups are kept. Each new one pushes the oldest out.
  • Find them under avatar → Settings → Backups. This works only in the web or desktop apps, not on mobile.
  • If you drop to the free plan, new backups stop, but the old ones remain available to download.

Each backup is a ZIP with one CSV file per active project. To bring a project back:

  1. Download a backup dated before the loss (that's what the Reporting date is for).
  2. Unzip it and find the CSV for the project you need.
  3. Create a new, empty project in Todoist.
  4. Open its three-dots menu, choose Manage data, then Import from CSV, and upload the file.
  5. If you only needed one or two tasks, move them back to the original project and delete the temporary one.

Know the limits before you start: backups leave out completed tasks and archived projects, keep up to 500 comments per task, and store attachments as links. Twenty-one backups only cover about three weeks of active days. If you noticed the loss later than that, the copy you need may already have rotated out.

4. Contact Todoist support

If you're on the free plan, if the loss is older than your oldest backup, or if you think a sync bug ate the tasks, write to Todoist support through their help center. Give them the project name, roughly when you last saw the tasks, and the date from Reporting. Specifics are much easier for them to act on than "everything disappeared." They can't promise recovery, but they're the only people who can see their side of it.

5. Keep your own copy from now on

Whatever happens this time, the fix for next time is the same: a backup that lives somewhere you control and doesn't age out after 21 days. You have a few options.

  • Export by hand. Any project's three-dots menu can export a CSV. Free, but only as reliable as your habit.
  • Write a script. Todoist's API is public and documented. If you're comfortable with a few dozen lines of code and a cron job, you can save your tasks on a schedule yourself.
  • Use a tool built for it. That's the gap UnDeleted was written for.

About UnDeleted

I wrote UnDeleted after losing my own tasks. It's a small open-source command-line tool that works with Todoist as well as Asana and ClickUp. On a schedule, it pulls your tasks with your own API token and commits them to a git repo you own, so every snapshot is kept for as long as you keep the repo. When something goes missing, undeleted restore compares a past snapshot with your live list and recreates only what's gone. It never edits or deletes existing tasks.

Where it falls short: it captures active tasks, not completed ones, and it doesn't save comments or attachments yet. It also can't recover anything deleted before its first snapshot ran, so it only helps if you set it up ahead of time. It's brand new, and nobody has paid for it yet.

Running it from source is free. Prebuilt binaries are $29 once or $19 a year.
